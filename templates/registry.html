The series

Each Argo series is one stone and one ceiling, declared in advance and never reopened. Settings are the owner's choice and are recorded in the registry.

The registry publishes the standing count of every stone in every series — issued, reserved, retired, and demonstration. The census is the whole population. There are no other Argo diamonds.

The covenants

The rules of the edition are published before the first stone is sold, and each certificate repeats them. They are not policies. They are commitments the house has placed beyond its own reach.

The series is closed.
Each year-series states its ceiling in advance. Serials within a series are issued only in that series' year; a serial not issued in its year is retired permanently and recorded here as retired. No retired serial is ever revived.
The lowest numbers are never offered.
Serials 001–005 of the First Series are reserved to the house archive. Demonstration stones carry D-series serials, are flagged at creation, and are never sold.
Every stone is inscribed and recorded.
Each stone's girdle carries its serial. Each serial's record here carries the stone's grading report number, conformance certificate, and setting record, for the life of the stone.
The book is audited.
An independent CPA firm examines the registry annually under agreed-upon procedures and publishes its attestation letter here. The design of the cut is not part of any public record.
The certificate is bonded.
Each stone's certificate is sworn before a notary, recorded with the county, backed by surety bond, and enforceable as an express warranty. The bond number appears on the certificate.
The book names stones, never owners.
No owner, buyer, or price appears in any public record, ever. Transfers are recorded as events in a stone's history, not as identities. The census counts stones; it does not count people.

The authorized jeweler

Every Argo diamond is sold through an authorized door. The registry records each authorization — holder, effective date, and standing — for the life of the program.

%AUTH_CARDS%

What authorization means: issued stones are sold only through the authorization(s) named on this page for their series; authorized benches execute the recorded settings; demonstration stones are shown by appointment through authorized doors. Each stone's record names the authorization under which it was issued. Authorizations are never removed from this page — a concluded authorization remains in the book with its dates, exactly as a retired serial does.

A seller who does not appear on this page has never been authorized. A private sale outside an authorized door is verified by an authorized jeweler at the time of sale: the stone is presented, confirmed against its record, and the transfer is recorded. A stone offered without this verification should be checked against the registry before anything else.

How verification works

Nothing in the Argo program asks for trust. Each claim is checkable against a party who does not answer to the house.

The stone
The girdle inscription matches this registry and the stone's independent grading report. Checkable with any jeweler's loupe and the lab's own report-lookup service.
The geometry
Each stone passes a scan-verified conformance test before issuance; the certificate states the pass and its date. The scan itself is sealed in the house archive — conformance is attested, geometry is never published.
The counts
The census on this site is examined annually by an independent CPA firm; the attestation letter is published here. The firm is named; the letter is addressed for public distribution.
The covenants
Each certificate is sworn, county-recorded, surety-bonded, and enforceable as an express warranty. Certified copies are available from the county clerk by anyone, forever.

The single exception to publication is the design of the cut itself — its angles, arrangement, and scan files — which is protected intellectual property and is never part of any public record. Everything else is upfront.

The Extract of the Registry

Beyond the public record, the house issues a formal Extract of the Registry — a sealed document reciting a stone's complete recorded history — to the stone's owner, their estate, or their insurer, upon request and verification of the stone.

One extract may be issued per stone in any twelve-month period. The extract recites the registry's records; it is not itself the bonded certificate, which travels with the stone from issuance. Whether an extract is issued rests with the house registrar alone. This public page is a record of standing, not a warranty document.
